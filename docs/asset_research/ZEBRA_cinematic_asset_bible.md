## Art direction

### Art direction lock

Stylized cinematic semi-realism. Stray is the reference for intimate, cluttered, lived-in alleys and animal-scale environmental storytelling; Cyberpunk 2077 for vertical megacity layers, signage, industrial scale and urban lighting. Neither game supplies assets or exact designs. The visual anchor remains IMG-01, not a mandate to reproduce copyrighted characters. Hoot is a human-bodied character with an owl head; Zebra! is a normal quadrupedal zebra. Both are custom hero assets, not low-poly replacements.

### Composition and density

Build one playable street district with two narrow alleys, a wider chase artery, a utility tunnel and a rooftop vista. Use inaccessible upper floors, silhouettes, fog and skybox geometry to suggest a huge city. Spend detail where camera and gameplay dwell; do not build an open-world city.

### Surface language

Physically based materials with visible wear at contact points: layered asphalt, chipped concrete, rust, wet metal, textured leather and restrained feathers. Use material variation and bevels rather than excessive geometry. Signs, pipes, ACs, wires, vents and litter create believable construction history. Avoid clean plastic toy surfaces, uniform chrome, overdone grunge and saturated neon on every surface.

### Color and light

Continuum: charcoal #101820, slate #273237, green #28D887, teal #377B79; sparks/alarms #E97A36 and red #D62C41. Reserve warm amber #C59052 for inhabited spaces and the bar; off-white #DDD5C6 highlights faces. Introduce emerald code mainly at facades, skyline and transitions. Keep faces, zebra stripes and routes visible under neutral key light.

### Camera and animation

Third-person lower, behind-and-offset camera in alleys; wider chase framing for hoof placement, obstacles and rider. Use deliberate cinematic closeups for jokes, strong silhouette language and short slow-motion inserts; no constant depth of field or motion blur during interactive play. Keep cinematic comedy earnest, not cartoon slapstick.

### Design ownership

Use original robot faceplates, silhouettes, lettering, symbols and fictional ads. Avoid recognizable Terminator endoskeleton copies, Matrix logo/code, actor likenesses, Stray/Cyberpunk models or extracted game content. Treat supplied concept images as internal visual references unless rights for public display are established.

## Technical direction

### Renderer

Desktop Godot 4.x Forward+; pin actual stable version at project start. 1080p/60 fps is an acceptance target, not an achieved benchmark. Use SSR only as a complement to reflection probes: screen-space reflections cannot see offscreen objects. Enable SSAO, carefully limited volumetric fog and HDR glow; use local lights to fake bounce if required. Tone mapping and exposure should be tested on both protagonists.

### Materials

Godot glTF 2.0 preferred; convert Blender/FBX sources to GLB with baked textures. Assign OpenGL +Y normals; preserve UV scale, metallic/roughness and AO. Use external material overrides and wrapper scenes so reimport is nondestructive. Wetness mask blends roughness (dry ~0.65-0.9, puddles ~0.08-0.25, artistic starting ranges), darkens asphalt and retains normal detail. Use opaque wet road material instead of stacked transparent planes.

### Lighting and effects

Use 1-2 shadowed hero lights per street segment as a starting budget; most fixtures use emissive materials and unshadowed local lights. Bake static lighting where appropriate; select one primary GI strategy after profiling. Particle-based rain and smoke, scripted puddle ripples, recycled sparks/explosions. Digital code on emissive facade strips and SubViewport billboards, with original type and glyphs. Avoid full-screen distracting flashes; add reduced flash/shake settings.

### Geometry and performance

Use modular snap grids per kit; unify scale at 1 unit = 1 m. Build low-complexity colliders and navigation independently. Suggested starting budgets, not hard limits: 50-90k triangles Hoot, 35-70k Zebra, 15-35k close robots, 1-10k street props; distant variants much lower. 2K textures for most near assets, 1K for props, selective 4K hero textures. Use visibility ranges, LODs, occlusion and instancing for recurring props. Profile actual CPU/GPU frametimes and VRAM at 1080p; set fallback for fog/SSR/shadows.

### Hero pipeline

Custom meshes from Blender and optional Tripo, then topology, UVs, textures, skeletons, weights and LODs. Hoot uses human skeleton and owl head, independent eye/beak shape keys/rig, coat bones or baked secondary motion, hand/beak sockets. Zebra uses equine rig; no humanoid retarget for quadruped. Make separate gait clips; sync riding root and gait phase with saddle socket and feet/reins IK. Keep mount animation and drinking upper-body layer independent. Test walk/trot/canter/gallop, turning, stops, mount and dismount.

### Asset intake

Use raw vendor archive outside runtime; license copy and provenance ledger, hash and derived paths. Import 2-3 pieces in a sandbox project before buying additional kits. Verify scale/pivots, mesh topology, material maps, collision, animated skeleton, LODs and clarity in rain. Restrict paid files to licensed collaborators and avoid public repos. Do not feed Unity store files or NoAI-tagged products into AI tools; check exact contract even for itch/ArtStation. Codex can operate on original code and metadata without ingesting restricted asset bytes.

## Production gates

### Gate 1 — look development

Create one 30-50 m alley with free AD-01/08 assets, simple wet asphalt, code billboard, rain and one moving light. Put greyboxed Hoot/Zebra at camera scale. Capture street-height and chase-camera screenshots; accept only if lighting reveals character silhouettes.

### Gate 2 — first purchase

If alley look passes, evaluate DEEZL AD-02 as city kit. Confirm license, download, convert and test three facades plus signs. A single city shell must support both intimate and broad chase framing. Spend $19.99 only after preview check.

### Gate 3 — vertical slice

Block 3-5 minutes: alley start, street chase, obstacle jump, drone flyby, robot ambush, slow-motion wine spill and tunnel exit. Test Hoot/Zebra rigs at gait transitions with gameplay camera; use simple stand-ins until rigs are finished.

### Gate 4 — expand

Only after vertical slice runs acceptably, evaluate AD-04 free sample or AD-03 industrial kit, and AD-05 robots. Reuse city geometry for assault and final scenes. Add original character-specific art, audio and environmental storytelling; do not buy multiple redundant city packs.
